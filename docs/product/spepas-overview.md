## What is SpePas?

SpePas is a one-stop online marketplace for car parts in Ghana, designed to connect buyers who need car parts with verified sellers in markets like Abossey Okai, coordinated through a network of mechanics, agents, and riders.

The long-term vision is to digitize and formalize the auto parts supply chain across West Africa — starting with Ghana's two dominant hubs (Abossey Okai, Accra and Suame Magazine, Kumasi) — and expand into a platform that also promotes the circular economy by facilitating the resale of used and refurbished parts.

SpePas is more than a marketplace. Its founding ambition is to:
- **Empower** sellers, mechanics, and gig workers through a structured digital economy
- **Disintermediate** price-inflating middlemen while keeping mechanics as paid, loyal partners
- **Build trust** in a market historically plagued by counterfeits, opaque pricing, and no delivery infrastructure
- **Create jobs** through its gig economy model (MEPAs, GOPAs, Riders)

**Market research (100 buyers + 100 sellers surveyed, 10 in-depth interviews)** confirms strong demand:
- **78% of buyers** purchase used parts occasionally or always — validating SpePas's focus on used parts
- **88% of sellers** want to reach customers in other regions but have no way to do so today
- **68% of buyers** believe intermediaries (mechanics, goro boys) inflate their costs
- **48% of sellers** report customers getting lost trying to find their shops
- **80% of buyers** currently self-pickup parts — delivery is a massive convenience unlock
- **42% of buyers** are dissatisfied or unsure about the current buying experience
- **85% of buyers** discover sellers through mechanic referrals — validating the MEPA model

## GTM Journey — Historical Timeline

Understanding how the GTM strategy evolved is essential context for current product and market decisions. The ideas were not born complete — they were refined through research, partner conversations, VC feedback, and hands-on operations planning.

### Phase 1 — Founding Vision (Pre-2024)

The earliest strategic documents frame SpePas as a platform to bring Ghana's auto parts markets online:

- **Core thesis**: Ghana's high vehicle import rate + poor road conditions = frequent repairs, but the parts market is entirely offline and fragmented.
- **Initial framing** (Biz Value Prop V1): SpePas would empower Abossey Okai sellers by bringing them online, create gig income for mechanics (MEPAs) and delivery workers, and promote the circular economy through used parts resale.
- **Early monetization thinking**: Transaction fees and subscriptions — with first 20 sellers getting a free year to seed the marketplace.
- **West Africa expansion** flagged from day one — *"pioneering the digital transformation of the auto parts industry in West Africa, staying ahead of competitors by 2–5 years."*

### Phase 2 — Competitive Positioning & Seller Strategy (Early 2025)

By Q1 2025, the team had done enough market analysis to articulate SpePas's clear differentiation from incumbents:

**vs. Jiji and Tonaton (dominant classifieds)**:

| Feature | Jiji / Tonaton | SpePas |
|---|---|---|
| Seller verification | None — anyone can list | Strict vetting & certification |
| Product authenticity | Rampant counterfeits | Guaranteed quality with warranties |
| Transaction security | Cash-based, high scam risk | Secured payments with escrow |
| Logistics | Buyers arrange own pickup | Integrated delivery with tracking |
| Customer support | Minimal or nonexistent | Dedicated support + dispute resolution |
| Community | None | Mechanics (MEPA), sellers, fleet operators |

The team's position: *"Jiji and Tonaton have failed to address the trust deficit in Ghana's auto parts market. SpePas will win by specializing in auto parts, enforcing strict verification, securing payments, and integrating logistics."*

**The Uncle Amos Seller Partnership** — the founding GTM move:

A pivotal early insight was that SpePas could not scale cold. It needed a credible anchor seller. Uncle Amos (Amos / shareholder partner) emerged as the ideal MVP 0 pioneer:
- Started auto parts business in **1990 via direct importation**; runs a computer-cataloged, well-inventoried shop
- Deep credibility in the **Mercedes sub-vertical** (prestige, 40+ year demographic)
- Has an existing **mechanic referral network** that can drive both seller and buyer adoption
- Values mechanic education and high-quality service delivery
- Skeptical of the stagnation in informal Abossey Okai markets — open to innovation

The strategic logic: *"Like mobile money didn't eliminate cash — it extended trust and reach. SpePas is doing the same for parts: from Abossey to API."*

Uncle Amos was positioned as a **"poster partner"** — his participation would reduce hesitation from other sellers and mechanics. His sub-vertical strategy for GTM:
- **Mercedes** (Amos's primary) → seed with Amos inventory and network
- **Toyota** → leverage Amos's network of sellers
- **KIA** → high volume of un-inventoried parts; GOPAs needed for order accuracy
- **Ford / Honda** → expand as market matures

**Metrics the team was tracking at this stage**:
- Recurring users
- Vertical expansion by manufacturer
- # of sellers from Amos's referral network
- # of mechanics engaging via Amos linkages

### Phase 3 — The MEPA Problem & Solution (Q1 2025)

The mechanics question was the hardest strategic knot to untie. The team documented this explicitly:

**The Challenge**: Mechanics in Ghana currently earn **5%–15% markup** by sourcing parts via informal contacts at Abossey Okai. Removing them from the supply chain would:
1. Alienate a critical influencer group (85% of buyers discover sellers through mechanics)
2. Disrupt a real income stream they depend on
3. Create resistance that could kill adoption

**SpePas's Behavioural Economics–Driven Response**:

Rather than remove mechanics, SpePas **rewards and integrates them** into the digital model. The design applies:
- **Loss aversion** — MEPAs don't lose their role; they gain extra income and recognition
- **Endowment effect** — integrating their current workflow into SpePas gives them ownership
- **Social proof** — MEPAs who succeed influence others to join
- **Status and exclusivity** — tiered membership creates aspirational value
- **Gamification** — points, ranks, tools make participation engaging

**The MEPA Tier System (Platinum → Gold → Silver → Bronze → Starter)**:
- Unique MEPA ID tracks all referral transactions
- Earn **10 GHC per order** placed through their referral
- Loyalty points redeemable for branded items (overalls, tools, keychains)
- Long-term: eligibility for SpePas-backed shop location support
- Top-performing MEPAs featured as **"Recommended Mechanics"** on the platform
- MEPAs generate invoices for customers — creating price transparency that also builds their credibility

**The Intel Inside Analogy** (from GTM MEPA Engagement doc): SpePas envisioned MEPA shops branded with *"Powered by SpePas"* stickers — just as Intel's logo on computers signaled quality, the SpePas mark on a mechanic's shop would signal verified sourcing and quality service. *"One step behind every quality service provided by the mechanic, whose parts are sourced from SpePas."*

**Initial MEPA Pilot Plan** (before full digital platform):
1. Mechanic sends request via **WhatsApp or phone**
2. Biz Dev team responds and confirms
3. Request passed to GOPA (procurement)
4. Item sourced and delivered
5. Payment collected via link or cash
6. Follow-up to ensure satisfaction

This phone/WhatsApp-first pilot approach was intentional — it mirrored mechanics' existing workflow (calling Abossey Okai riders), making adoption frictionless.

### Phase 4 — VC Mentor Input & Quality-First Brand Promise (November 2024)

A strategic review session with VC mentor **Dapo** surfaced important refinements:

**Problems identified**:
- MEPA referrals were inadvertently inflating prices, reducing trust
- Lack of consistent quality assurance across the supply chain
- Need for clearer brand promise at all touchpoints

**Key shifts that came out of this session**:

1. **Quality-first brand promise** — SpePas guarantees quality parts delivered within a reliable timeframe. Buyers save time and avoid uncertainty by trusting SpePas to vet and deliver. *"Quality Product"* was crystallized as the brand promise.

2. **StockX-style parts classification**:
   - **New & Packaged**: Fully returnable
   - **Used / As-Is**: Tested by intermediate mechanics before listing; non-returnable but SpePas mediates disputes with sellers

3. **Returns mechanism**: SpePas sends a rider to pick up incorrect/defective parts at no cost to the buyer. Sellers bear the cost of return logistics. Buyers receive **sealed parts** for authenticity and traceability.

4. **Buyer segmentation focus**: Prioritize **fleet buyers** and **long-term mechanic buyers** who value consistent quality, reliable delivery, and transparent pricing.

5. **Operations hires**: The mentor flagged the need for an Onsite Manager and Project Manager — someone to conduct field checks, provide weekly status reports, and maintain strategic focus beyond financial incentives.

### Phase 5 — Pricing Model Evolution (2024 → March 2025)

The pricing model went through documented iterations:

**V1 (rough notes, 2024)** — exploratory:
- First 20 sellers: 1-year free subscription to seed marketplace
- Transaction fee: flat **20 GHC** per transaction (split: 10 GHC GOPA + 10 GHC MEPA)
- Alternative: 1% of item price, min 10 GHC cap
- MEPA benefit: 10 GHC per order + referral bonuses with reward points → gift items

**V2 (cleaned up, 2024)** — formalized same model with a max cap:
- Flat model: **20 GHC** split (10 GHC GOPA / 10 GHC MEPA)
- Percentage model: 1% of item price, **min 10 GHC / max 100 GHC**
- Fee only applies if GOPA or MEPA was involved — direct transactions carry no fee

**GTM V1 (VC Mentor session notes, March 2025)** — refined principles:
- Subscription model: annual fee TBD post-GTM performance measurement
- SpePas **will not inflate part prices** — customer-facing price transparency is a non-negotiable trust principle
- MEPA incentive program formalized: referral IDs, cumulative earnings, loyalty rewards (branded gifts, long-term land acquisition support)
- Returns at no cost to buyer; **sellers cover return logistics**

### Phase 6 — Platform Build & Operations Readiness (Jul–Aug 2025)

By mid-2025, the platform had moved into active development with a concrete tech team and PM oversight. Key decisions from this period:

**Access channel strategy finalized**:
- **MVP 0** (priority): Phone call + USSD (`*887*9#`) — no data, no smartphone required
- **MVP 1**: Full web app at spepas.com
- **Mobile app**: de-scoped from MVP; USSD and web are the launch vehicles
- **WhatsApp**: used for early MEPA pilot and informal comms; not a formal platform channel

**USSD architecture decisions** (from Seller Bid Final Review and USSD meetings, Jul 2025; updated Mar 2026):
- **Shortcode** `*887*9#` for all roles (buyer, seller, rider) via role-specific mocker endpoints
- **Channel rule:** Requests are created by phone (call-in) or website only — USSD is for accepting bids, paying, and tracking
- Seller USSD menu: Open Requests (All / By part / By brand) / My Bids / Contact Us
- Rider USSD menu: New pickups / In progress deliveries / Completed deliveries / Wallet / Contact Us
- Buyer USSD menu: My requests / Cart (Check Out, Remove Item) / Contact us
- Bid expiry: starting with 2–5 days (longer than initial 24hr proposal) to accommodate early user behaviour
- Quantity lock-in after bid accepted — prevents gaming discounts
- GOPA uses **web only** (no USSD); MEPA will be a **separate USSD flow** due to complexity

**Backend sequencing** (from Road to MVP Scope meeting, Aug 16 2025):

2. USSD Buyer → 1 week
3. USSD Seller → 2 weeks

*Total: ~8 weeks to USSD-ready state*

**Mobile app** (Jul 27 2025 meeting): Blocked on missing backend APIs. Static placeholders in place. Samuel Acheampong onboarding as frontend developer. Andrea Afi as PM "policing" MVP scope. Figma-driven task breakdown per screen.

## Problems Being Solved

- **Geographic concentration:** The entire auto parts market is concentrated in two hubs — Abossey Okai (Accra) and Suame Magazine (Kumasi). Buyers across Ghana must travel to these locations or rely on intermediaries. *"That is the available place for all parts. You don't have any option."* — Buyer interview
- **Price opacity and intermediary markups:** 68% of buyers believe they spend more due to third parties. Goro boys are tipped from both sides (78% of buyers believe this), and car owners receive inflated prices compared to trade buyers. *"When they see you as the car owner, they give you inflated prices."* — Buyer interview
- **Information asymmetry:** Buyers cannot verify part quality or authenticity. *"Normally it is brought in a box so I assume it is new. But if I am being deceived, I wouldn't know."* — Buyer interview
- **No digital presence for sellers:** 36% of sellers lose customers due to their physical location. 28% of shops are not close to the main road. Sellers have no way to be discovered online.
- **Time cost for buyers:** Working professionals cannot spend hours at the market. *"If you have a problem with your car, you can't be at the shop and be at the office at the same time."* — Buyer interview
- **No delivery infrastructure:** 80% of buyers physically collect their parts. There is no structured delivery system in the market.

## Challenges to Overcome

- Lack of standardized inventory systems among sellers
- Mechanics earning 5–15% markup on parts sourcing — must be converted to partners, not bypassed
- Risk of buyers bypassing the platform to deal directly with sellers
- Cash-dominant economy — sellers prefer cash over mobile money due to transaction charges and fraud concerns
- Order tagging and fulfillment process management for sellers
- Connectivity gaps — only 12% of buyers have tried any digital channel; USSD-first is essential
- Counterfeit/inferior parts flooding the market, eroding buyer trust
- Difficulty identifying part details without standardized naming (Geo-based aliases for part names)
- VIN/Chassis number lookup needed to normalize part identification across regions

## User Roles

SpePas has six distinct user roles, each with their own app experience:

| Role | Description |
|---|---|
| **Buyer** | Individuals or mechanics searching for and purchasing car parts |
| **Seller** | Used/new parts store owners (e.g. Abossey Okai market vendors) listing inventory and fulfilling orders |
| **GOPA** | Wholesale partners / Ground Operations Parts Aggregators who onboard sellers and broker requests between buyers and their seller network |
| **MEPA** | Mechanic Partners who search and order parts on behalf of their customers, earning referral commissions |
| **Rider** | Delivery agents who pick up parts from sellers and deliver to buyers |
| **Admin** | SpePas staff who manage users, orders, analytics, and platform configuration |

A single user account can hold **multiple roles**. Switching between active roles requires OTP verification.

## Access Channels

### Phone Call (MVP0 — Primary)
Buyers place orders by calling a SpePas phone line staffed by human agents (up to 5–6 concurrent lines via **3CX**). The agent captures vehicle details and parts, creates the request in the Admin Portal, and sends the buyer an SMS with a tracking code + USSD instructions. **PIN is never collected on phone calls** — bid acceptance and checkout happen exclusively in USSD.

### USSD / SMS (MVP0 — Primary)
Accessible from any mobile phone by dialling `*887*9#` — no data, no app, no smartphone required. **Requests are created by phone (call-in) or website only** — USSD is used to accept bids, make payments, and track orders. The USSD interface handles bid review, cart, checkout (with PIN + MoMo), delivery tracking, and wallet management via sequential numbered menus. Each session is stateful: the system remembers where a user is in a flow across menu selections. Delivery confirmation uses a **numeric delivery code** (given to buyer at checkout) instead of QR scanning.

USSD access is available for: **Buyer, Seller, Rider** (all via `*887*9#`).
- **USSD mockers** for testing: `mocker.spepas.com/` (buyer), `mocker.spepas.com/seller`, `mocker.spepas.com/rider`
- **GOPA** uses web only (no USSD)

### Web App (MVP1)
Full-featured React web app at spepas.com. Requires a smartphone with internet access. Supports all roles and all flows. Uses QR code scanning for delivery confirmation. **GOPA uses web only** — no USSD for GOPA.

### WhatsApp (Early Pilot / Pre-Platform)
Used in the initial MEPA pilot: mechanics send part requests via WhatsApp; Biz Dev team responds and transcribes orders manually. This mimics the existing mechanic workflow (calling Abossey Okai riders) to minimize friction during early validation. Not a formal long-term platform channel.

## Key User Flows

### Buyer — Phone Order (MVP0 Primary Flow)
1. Buyer calls SpePas phone line (via 3CX)
2. Agent identifies caller (existing profile or creates minimal profile)
3. Agent collects vehicle details: year, brand, model, drivetrain/fuel
4. Agent maps parts to catalog codes using Code Builder widget in Admin Portal
5. Agent reads back order summary: "You requested [Year Brand Model], parts: [list]"
6. Agent creates request in Admin Portal (one per part type, source = Phone)
7. System sends SMS to buyer: "Request R48291 created. Review bids via `*887*9#` → My requests." + link to part images
8. GOPA forwards request to specific sellers in their network
9. Sellers receive the request and submit bids via USSD (Open Requests) or web
10. Buyer receives SMS when bids arrive: "New bids for R48291. Dial `*887*9#` → My requests"
11. Buyer accepts bid via USSD (My requests → select → accept) or web → item added to Cart
12. Buyer checks out via USSD (Cart → Check Out → MoMo payment) or web
13. Seller marks order ready → Rider picks up (USSD: New pickups) → Rider delivers using delivery code

---

### Buyer — Purchasing a Listed Part (Direct / Inventory)
1. Buyer searches for a part by car make/model/year (e.g. "2014 Toyota Vitz spark plug")
2. Part is found in the catalogue → buyer sees available sellers and prices
3. Buyer selects a seller, adds to cart
4. Buyer enters delivery address and payment details, checks out
5. Mobile money payment notification sent to buyer for approval
6. Seller fulfils the order
7. Rider accepts pickup job and navigates to seller location
8. Buyer receives status updates: order fulfilled → rider en route with ETA
9. Rider arrives at buyer; buyer scans rider's QR code in app to confirm delivery
10. Buyer rates the delivery experience and later rates the seller

**USSD channel:** Requests are created by phone (call-in) or website only. Buyers use USSD to accept bids, check out, and track orders.

---

### Buyer — Requesting a Non-Listed Part (Bidding)
1. Buyer submits a request for a part not in the catalogue via phone call-in or website (car make/model/year + part description + quantity)
2. GOPA forwards request to specific sellers in their network
3. Sellers submit bids with price, delivery date, and optionally photos
4. Buyer receives notification and reviews bids
5. Buyer selects a bid, adds to cart, checks out
6. Rider picks up and delivers; buyer confirms with QR scan
7. Buyer rates delivery and seller

**Note**: Quantity is locked after bid acceptance — buyers cannot change quantity to game discounts. A new request must be submitted for a different quantity.

**USSD channel:** Request creation is phone/web only. Buyers use USSD to view bids, accept, check out, and track.

---

### Buyer — GOPA-Brokered Request
1. Buyer submits a part request (via phone call-in or website)
2. GOPA forwards the request to specific sellers in their network
3. Sellers submit bids; buyer receives notification and selects an offer
4. Checkout, delivery, and QR confirmation follow the standard flow

---

### MEPA — Purchasing on Behalf of a Customer
1. Mechanic (MEPA) searches for the required part and submits a request
2. MEPA receives offers and selects one
3. MEPA creates an invoice and forwards it to the customer's SpePas account (MEPA = "Buyer Pro Max" — creates invoices for others to pay)
4. Customer retrieves invoice via USSD menu and pays manually (push-to-pay avoided for security)
5. MEPA tracks order fulfilment and delivery on behalf of the customer
6. Delivery confirmed by buyer via QR code scan
7. MEPA earns referral commission tracked via unique MEPA ID

---

### Seller — Submitting a Bid (Web)
1. Seller views requests assigned by their GOPA (or unassigned requests)
2. Seller confirms stock availability
3. Seller submits a bid: unit price, max quantity, optional discount, expected delivery date, optional photos
4. Seller monitors bid status (pending, accepted, rejected)
5. Bid expires after 2–5 days (early rollout; shorter expiry possible later)

### Seller — Submitting a Bid (USSD)
USSD shortcode: `*887*9#` | Mocker: `mocker.spepas.com/seller`

```
Seller Main Menu:
  1. Open Requests → 1. All Requests | 2. By part | 3. By brand
  2. My bids
  3. Contact us
```

1. Dial `*887*9#` → 1. Open Requests
2. Browse requests: 1. All Requests, 2. By part, or 3. By brand
3. Select a request → enter unit price → enter expected delivery date → confirm with PIN
4. Bid submitted; seller can view active bids under 2. My bids

### Seller — Marking an Item Ready for Pickup
1. After a buyer checks out (invoice created), seller receives notification
2. Seller prepares the part and packages it in a SpePas bag
3. Seller marks the item as "ready for pickup" via app or USSD
4. Item becomes visible to riders for pickup acceptance
5. Seller verifies the rider's credentials before handoff
6. Payment released to seller's wallet 24 hours after delivery is confirmed

**Via USSD:** `*887*9#` → 2. My bids → select accepted bid → mark as ready for pickup (PIN confirmation)

---

### GOPA — Onboarding Sellers, Forwarding Requests & Tracking Orders
1. GOPA onboards new sellers into their network (name, phone, store, location, payment details)
2. GOPA receives notification for a new buyer request
3. GOPA forwards the request to specific sellers in their network
4. GOPA monitors bid submissions from their sellers
5. After buyer checks out, order proceeds to fulfilment and rider pickup
6. GOPA tracks the status of forwarded orders (bid received → accepted → shipped → delivered)

*GOPA does not have a USSD flow — web only.*

### Invoice Flow (Cross-Role)
The invoice is the central document that links checkout to fulfilment:

```
Buyer checks out (web or USSD Cart → Check Out)
  → Invoice created (PENDING)
  → Seller marks items ready (READY_TO_BE_PICKED)
  → Rider accepts pickup job (USSD: New pickups)
  → Rider picks up from seller (QR scan at seller)
  → In transit (SHIPPED)
  → Rider delivers to buyer (delivery code or QR scan)
  → Invoice closed (DELIVERED)
  → Seller and rider wallets credited
```

Status lifecycle: `PENDING → RECEIVED → WARE_HOUSE → READY_TO_BE_SHIPPED → READY_TO_BE_PICKED → SHIPPED → DELIVERED` (or `CANCELLED / FAILED`)

---

### Rider — Pickup and Delivery (Web)
1. Rider views available pickup jobs (items marked ready by sellers)
2. Rider accepts a job; pickup address shown on Google Maps
3. Rider navigates to seller location
4. Seller verifies rider identity; rider scans QR code at seller location to confirm pickup
5. Rider navigates to buyer address (shown on map)
6. Rider delivers; buyer scans rider's QR code to confirm delivery
7. Rider uploads a photo as proof of delivery
8. Rider's wallet is credited after delivery is confirmed

### Rider — Pickup and Delivery (USSD)
USSD shortcode: `*887*9#` | Mocker: `mocker.spepas.com/rider`

```
Rider Main Menu:
  1. New pickups           → accept available pickup jobs
  2. In progress deliveries → track active deliveries, enter delivery code
  3. Completed deliveries   → view delivery history
  4. Wallet                 → total balance / available to withdraw / lifetime earnings
     └── 1. Withdraw → enter 4-digit PIN
  5. Contact us
```

1. Dial `*887*9#` → 1. New pickups → browse available jobs → select → confirm
2. After pickup: 2. In progress deliveries → select delivery → update status
3. On delivery: enter buyer's delivery code to confirm → moves to 3. Completed deliveries
4. Check earnings: 4. Wallet → view balances → 1. Withdraw → enter PIN

3. Rider selects 1. Withdraw

## USSD Flow Detail

The USSD interface is a **state-machine menu system** — each screen is an activity with numbered options. Users navigate by entering a number and pressing send. Sessions are stateful; a user can exit and resume.

### Navigation conventions
- Enter a display number (e.g. `1`, `2`, `3`) to select an option
- Enter `0#` to return to the main menu
- Enter `1#` to go back to the previous screen
- Enter `#` to advance to the next page of a paginated list

### Buyer USSD main menu (`*887*9#`)
Mocker: `mocker.spepas.com`

**Note:** Requests are created by phone (call-in) or on the website — not via USSD. Once a request exists, buyers use USSD to accept bids, check out (MoMo payment), and track orders.

1. **Create request** — ⚠️ Still present in mocker but should be removed (requests via phone/web only)
2. **My requests** — View requests with active bids → select a request → view bids → select a bid → add to cart with PIN
3. **Cart** — 1. Check Out (MoMo payment) | 2. Remove Item
4. **Contact us** — Support escalation

### Seller USSD main menu (`*887*9#`)
Mocker: `mocker.spepas.com/seller`

1. **Open Requests** — 1. All Requests | 2. By part | 3. By brand
   - Select a request → enter unit price → enter expected delivery date → confirm with PIN (creates bid)
2. **My bids** — View accepted bids → select bid → mark as ready for pickup (PIN)
3. **Contact us** — Support escalation

### Rider USSD main menu (`*887*9#`)
Mocker: `mocker.spepas.com/rider`

1. **New pickups** — Browse available pickup jobs → select → confirm
2. **In progress deliveries** — Track active deliveries → enter delivery code to confirm
3. **Completed deliveries** — View delivery history
4. **Wallet** — Total balance / available to withdraw / lifetime earnings → 1. Withdraw → enter 4-digit PIN
5. **Contact us** — Support escalation

### MEPA USSD (separate flow)
- Separate dedicated flow due to complexity of invoice creation and forwarding
- Invoice creation → sharing → retrieval by buyer → manual payment confirmation (no push-to-pay)

## Payment & Wallet

- **Primary payment method:** Mobile money (MTN, Vodafone, AirtelTigo)
- **Payment gateway:** CalBank / CalPay (agreement signed; callback integration in progress as of Aug 2025)
- **Checkout flow:** Buyer enters wallet number and network at checkout; mobile money prompt sent for approval
- **Seller/Rider payouts:** Credited to in-app wallet after delivery confirmation (24-hour hold for sellers)
- **Withdrawal:** Users request a withdrawal from their wallet to a registered bank account or mobile money number
- **MEPA invoice payment:** Buyer manually retrieves invoice via USSD and pays — push-to-pay avoided for security

## QR Code System

QR codes are used at two points in every delivery:

| Point | Who scans | What it confirms |
|---|---|---|
| **Seller handoff** | Rider scans seller's QR in app | Correct items collected from correct seller |
| **Buyer delivery** | Buyer scans rider's QR in app | Correct items delivered to correct buyer |

QR codes are linked to invoices in the database. Scanning resolves the invoice/item and updates its status.
